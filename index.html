Bon appétit! French words on the English menu

Many English food words come from French. Read the short texts, tap the red words, listen to how they sound and answer the questions.

Why so many?

In 1066 the Normans from France took over England. For hundreds of years French was the language of the court and of rich kitchens. Many of those words stayed in English, and we still use them in restaurants today.

  • French u is Norwegian y. Say menu like “meny” and purée like “pyre”.
  • ch sounds like sj. Chef sounds like the Norwegian “sjef”.
  • Final letters are often silent. In croissant and restaurant the last t is quiet.
  • Nasal vowels. In an and on, send the sound through your nose and skip the n.
